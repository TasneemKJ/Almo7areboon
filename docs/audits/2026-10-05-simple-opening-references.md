# Almo7areboon: 20 mobile references for a simpler opening

Research date: 5 October 2026 (UTC). Project: TasneemKJ/Almo7areboon. Scope: a Levantine storybook, side-on auto-combat game with regenerating food, troop deployment, base defence/destruction, Gather, Hold/Advance, and three skills.

## Decision in brief

Use **We Are Warriors** for the food-to-deployment relationship, **The Battle Cats** for a clearly legible deploy/defend/destroy loop and staged teaching, and **Stick War: Legacy** for the distinction between growing an army and commanding its position. Use **Kingdom Two Crowns**, **Thronefall**, and **Bad North** as complementary references for an atmospheric world and fewer high-level decisions.

These are **20 relevance-ordered references available on mobile**, not a verified worldwide “top 20 of 2026.” Popularity and relevance are separate. No source establishes that copying these patterns will improve Almo retention. The reported early abandonment is owner feedback, not an analytics measurement.

The strongest proposed change is a distinct, quiet Home → meaningful battlefield interaction → short result loop. The current direction limits visible UI buttons/chrome to two or three; genuine, legible physical scene objects can remain directly interactive and must be audited separately. A seven-choice tactics popup is still a seven-choice task. Neither hiding it behind one button nor scattering disguised menu buttons across the scenery solves the complexity problem.

## Evidence rules and limits

- **D: documented** in a developer-authored store description or official site. This establishes advertised functionality, not an observed interaction sequence.
- **V: visually inspected** image. Six official We Are Warriors promotional images were downloaded and individually viewed. They are composites/cropped marketing material, not a first-launch recording.
- **U: unknown/unobserved.** No current native app was installed, bought, signed into, or played. Exact home-screen flows, first 120 seconds, tutorial gates, touch timings, and results-screen control counts are U for all 20 titles unless a narrower fact is explicitly documented below.
- **I: design inference/proposal.** The Almo applications are design judgments to test, not facts about the references.
- A screenshot URL, alt text, or video search result was not counted as visual observation. Official footage links are supplied as candidates and explicitly marked unviewed.

## Deeper examination of the three closest references

### 1. We Are Warriors: preserve the resource/action connection

**Entry:** U. The iOS release history documents onboarding work in December 2025 and further interface revisions through September 2026, but it does not establish the present number of opening gates. **First 120 seconds:** U. The developer documents food-based troop creation. Exact starting resources, unit availability and tutorial timing were not measured. **Core:** choose when to spend food on troops and pressure the opposing base. **Feedback:** the viewed promotional images depict troops, health/damage figures, an explosion and a large skill icon; these do not verify real live HUD counts. **Results:** U. No present victory/defeat screen was observed. [Official iOS listing and release history](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/we-are-warriors/id6466648550).

**Useful application to Almo (I):** The first meaningful success should be “I sent this troop and it is now defending us,” not “a message says I recruited something.” Place the real food cost on the relevant action, animate only after the canonical handler succeeds, and update the real resource display. An unaffordable action should explain the shortfall at the same location. Retain the real price, regeneration, cooldown and rewards.

**Do not copy:** the inspected store graphics include crowded card and weapon collections. They are evidence that the wider game can be dense, not proof that its opening is minimal. The two horde/barricade marketing images should not be reverse-engineered as the actual side-on first level.

### 2. The Battle Cats: teach the causal chain, then the exception

**Entry:** U visually. Current release notes explicitly locate vibration preferences on the title screen and in battle, establishing that settings can remain reachable in both contexts. **First 120 seconds:** U. Version 15.5.0, dated 27 July 2026 in the US App Store history, changed feature-unlock timing across early campaign stages; June notes also revised battle/item tutorials. **Core:** tap to deploy a cat, fire the cannon when needed, defeat the opposing base. **Feedback:** no gallery image was visually inspected in this pass. **Results:** stage completion awards XP/items according to official documentation; the actual result layout and button count are U. [Official listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/the-battle-cats/id850057092), [PONOS gameplay summary](https://www.ponos.jp/en/games/thebattlecats/).

**Useful application to Almo (I):** Teach deployment through the first encounter; introduce one relevant skill when its effect can be seen; introduce stance through the army visibly stopping or moving. Tie explanations to real events rather than arbitrary timers or an opening lecture. An advanced save must retain access to the complete existing toolset. Tutorial disclosure must not secretly become a new progression lock.

**Do not copy:** a store description saying “simple” is not evidence for three controls. Nor does a successful long-running collection game establish that Almo needs a large unit roster panel, gacha flow, daily-reward interruption or repeated tutorial gate.

### 3. Stick War: Legacy: separate production from orders

**Entry:** U. **First 120 seconds:** U. **Core:** officially documented mining, army production, formation control and enemy statue destruction. **Feedback:** the developer describes more legible hit effects, but they were not visually tested here. **Results:** difficulty crowns and campaign progression are documented; a short result screen is not established. The official [2019 mobile trailer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P2SwYviFJNY) and [2015 mobile trailer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u3umAofx9dc) were located but not watched. They are historical promotional evidence, not a current 2026 fresh-install walkthrough. [Official mobile listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.maxgames.stickwarlegacy&hl=en_US).

**Useful application to Almo (I):** Camp/resource decisions and battlefield orders should be distinguishable by location and language. A rally standard is an understandable place to issue a movement order only if it has a visible label, a comfortable target and a first-use demonstration. Changing stance must visibly alter the troop behaviour driven by existing game state. Do not leave a fixed “Advance” label on an action that now means “Hold.”

**Do not copy:** neither the extra individual-unit controls nor multiple modes are needed for Almo’s opening. A familiar strategy reference can inform the vocabulary without defining the amount of UI.

## Complementary lessons, with evidence boundaries

- **Kingdom Two Crowns / New Lands (D → I):** both official listings describe a side-scrolling economy and defensive settlement. This supports a world-centred metaphor for Almo. It does not establish that the mobile UI is literally buttonless or universally discoverable. Make interactive buildings visibly interactive; decorative world objects must not become undiscoverable shortcuts.
- **Thronefall (D → I):** the developer explicitly positions it as simplified strategy and separates building/economy from defence. Adopt clearer present-tense tasks, not an unapproved change to Almo’s simulation phases. A September 2026 Google Play review complains about holding a small gallop icon while moving. That is one user’s anecdote, not measured prevalence, but it is a useful warning against replacing visible actions with awkward hold gestures. [Mobile listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.doghowlgames.thronefall&hl=en_US).
- **Bad North (D → I):** its official description says the player issues broad tactical commands while soldiers handle navigation and engagement. Almo already has auto-combat; preserve that advantage. Simplify the decisions around it, rather than automating away the player’s existing strategic choices. [Official site](https://www.badnorth.com/).

## Proposed Almo flow: original design, not a copied reference

### Home

A distinct Levantine storybook scene: camp at dusk, one readable title, one line of chapter/location context, and one primary **Play** or **Continue**. Use existing original art and atmosphere rather than another game’s assets. Do not run the visible battlefield dashboard behind it.

Count every target. **Continue + Settings = two** is enough. If a third is genuinely needed, keep it secondary. Sound/language/accessibility remain reachable from Settings. Saved preferences must be honoured and sound must start after a real user gesture. Continue resumes the actual save. Restart belongs behind a clear, safe confirmation, never a misleading primary button.

No extra reward claim, mode hub, news feed, tutorial slideshow or forced roster review before ordinary play. Existing required notices and protection rules remain intact.

### Play: separate sparse chrome from a playable world

**Current interpretation:** the hard two–three limit applies to visible UI buttons/chrome. Real physical scene objects can remain directly interactive when their function is legible. Audit those semantic targets separately; do not disable genuine world interaction merely to force a total of three hit regions. Still present one obvious opening opportunity.

A baseline **Camp + Standard + Pause** could be three painted controls, but camp and standard are strongest when they are actual locations/objects already belonging to the game. An additional real gate, food source or troop may be directly actionable without being a fourth chrome button. This does not authorize scattering seven newly invented menu props around the canvas or an always-visible roster/power row.

Recommended structural rules:

1. Start with one obvious physical opportunity relevant to the immediate threat. A short label at the real camp or unit gives the verb and true cost; the player performs it.
2. Prefer direct world interaction where the object naturally owns the action. Recruitment belongs at the camp, Gather at the actual assembly point beside camp, and stance at the actual gate or rally standard. Keep semantic labels, focus states and comfortable touch areas even when there is no rectangular button.
3. Reveal a small contextual choice next to the selected object only when needed. Replace obsolete UI rather than layering it. A focus can show one or two choices plus Back; never seven tactics at once.
4. Distinguish a real object from a disguised navigation button: it should remain part of the physical scene, have a stable location and recognizable role, and change visibly when its action succeeds. A floating icon with a scenic skin remains chrome.
5. Map every existing troop and skill from both new and late saves before implementing. Preserve all three skills, Gather, stance and every legal troop choice. Contextual access must not let the system pick tactics or introduce new progression locks.
6. For multiple abilities associated with one real actor/object, use a small, labelled, bounded focus. Ground the grouping in the actual skill roster. A shallow branch is preferable to an endless carousel, but extra navigation is still a usability cost to test. Do not silently discard less-used options.
7. Never make an option available only through long press, a tiny icon, an invisible hotspot or an unexplained swipe. Give the player a visible, ordinary-tap route and accessible semantics. Avoid targets moving just before a tap.
8. Keep original simulation timing, prices and cooldowns unless separately approved. Opening a focus must not silently pause or accelerate combat and change balance.

This distinction removes the false requirement to funnel every real battlefield action through one master tactics menu. The player should act on a few understandable things in the world. Keep the scene sparse enough that the initial action is obvious, and audit every later-save object for legibility and overlap. The exact map requires the actual roster and existing skill identities; this report does not invent replacement mechanics or new scenery merely to hide buttons.

### Feedback

- **Deploy:** a successful canonical purchase produces the real unit at camp and visibly decreases food.
- **Gather:** newly deployed real troops wait at the existing assembly point; Release sends those gathered troops onward. It does not collect food or grant resources.
- **Hold/Advance:** actual formation movement and the standard’s state tell the player the command worked.
- **Skill:** world effect, true cooldown/readiness and the relevant unit/base response tell the story.
- **Failure:** the local action explains why it failed without spending resources or manufacturing success.
- Toasts are reserved for exceptional save/error/consent information, not the main explanation of each move.

### Results

Freeze or settle on the actual battlefield outcome. Show one clear Victory/Defeat title, the essential earned reward or next destination, and one primary **Continue** or **Retry**. At most two secondary actions, such as Details and Home, are allowed. Details is optional; the normal result must fit without scrolling. Do not fabricate rewards, skip costs, reset progression, or require a report gate after an event that previously continued the story.

## First-120-second Almo verification plan

This is an acceptance test to run on the build, not a claim that the reference games were tested.

1. Record a real clean-profile Home and ordinary Play input. Count visible UI/chrome targets against the two–three cap. Separately inventory genuine physical world targets, their semantics and discoverability; distinguish them from disguised menu buttons.
2. Record the first meaningful action, its input timestamp, the resulting canonical state change, and its visible battlefield effect. No injected save or scripted tutorial skipping.
3. Continue through at least 120 actual seconds of interaction. Log when each mechanic becomes relevant, the prompt shown, the player’s action and the control replacement.
4. Test an unaffordable action, an available skill, a cooldown, Gather and both stance states. Check that feedback stays local and real.
5. Check Pause, ordinary victory, defeat/retry, return to Home and a reload/Continue. Confirm save/cost/reward equality with the existing rules.
6. Repeat with representative later saves and all existing choices. A clean tutorial alone cannot pass this requirement.
7. Check the shipped English locale (Almo does not currently ship Arabic), 320px portrait, normal portrait, short landscape, rotation, touch, keyboard, screen-reader naming, reduced motion and safe areas. Check the UI/chrome cap separately from the physical-object inventory; all actionable world objects still need legible affordances, semantic names and non-overlapping touch areas.
8. Keep the agreed 500 KiB shipped gzip-JavaScript budget and existing protection rules. This research provides no authority to change them.
