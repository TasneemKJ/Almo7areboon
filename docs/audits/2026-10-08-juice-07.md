# Juice iteration 7: Camera impulse priority and recovery

## Current design review — 2026-10-08

This concrete alternative review expands the earlier short implementation brainstorm. It was written during current review; it does not claim these detailed alternatives were recorded before the source work.

**Identify:** crowded heavy impacts and major combat beats needs a readable physical reply. **Define:** attach the reply to the existing battlefield camera; preserve the existing rules, art, controls and save formats. **Explore:** the twenty concrete alternatives below. **Act:** retain the selected small response in the existing renderer. **Look back:** behavior counterchecks and source integration checks support this implementation; mandatory viewed mobile/browser acceptance is still pending because Chromium cannot start in this environment.

**Who:** the phone strategist watching live combat. **What:** Camera impulse priority and recovery. **Where:** the existing battlefield camera. **When:** crowded heavy impacts and major combat beats, then a finite reply. **Why:** strengthen cause and effect without another menu, toast or persistent panel.

| # | Concrete alternative | Decision and reason |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A short priority cooldown admits one stronger cut-in | Selected: preserves impact hierarchy and camera recovery |
| 2 | Every heavy hit forces a new camera shake | Rejected: creates continuous jitter in crowded fights |
| 3 | Heavy deaths disable base-hit shakes briefly | Deferred: A general priority limiter is easier to keep coherent |
| 4 | Camera shake amplitude scales with damage | Deferred: Hidden tuning should not automatically exaggerate motion |
| 5 | Meteor alone receives camera motion | Deferred: Would remove useful heavy/base contact already in the game |
| 6 | Victory uses a slower settling camera | Deferred: A new camera trajectory needs browser art review |
| 7 | Camera rotates by a fraction of a degree | Deferred: Rotation can impair phone targeting and text readability |
| 8 | Impulses accumulate in a damped spring | Deferred: More persistent camera state than this bounded limiter needs |
| 9 | Horizontal shake replaces two-axis shake | Deferred: Needs independent device-level visual acceptance |
| 10 | Enemy base damage gets a longer shake | Deferred: Would reduce hierarchy relative to actual player danger |
| 11 | Ordinary impacts restart only after recovery | Selected through the same cooldown admission rule |
| 12 | Higher-priority events cut in once with force | Selected: Phaser’s default non-forcing call cannot express that hierarchy |
| 13 | Camera remains motionless while reduced | Selected: motion preference suppresses admission |
| 14 | A static edge glint replaces reduced shake | Deferred: Existing hit/base cues already retain meaning |
| 15 | Hit-stop and shake share a single timer | Deferred: They solve different presentation needs and already have distinct ownership |
| 16 | Shake cooldown uses wall-clock time | Rejected: pause and replacement should retain battle ownership |
| 17 | Scene replacement resets the live shake effect | Selected: no inherited kick on a replacement battlefield |
| 18 | Each lane owns a separate camera nudge | Deferred: A single camera cannot truthfully focus three independent impulses |
| 19 | Unit deaths pan toward the fallen actor | Deferred: Could move controls away from the player’s thumb |
| 20 | A Settings strength slider controls impulses | Deferred: Adds persistent preference UI beyond the requested world response |
